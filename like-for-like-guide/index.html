Like-for-Like Guide

Align purpose, specs, quantity, included parts, and current page context before comparing.

Comparison begins with alignment

Like-for-like means the two things you set side by side are the same kind of thing before you read any difference between them. A comparison made too early reads the gap between two different options, not the difference between two comparable ones.

Step 1 — Align the purpose

Confirm the candidates belong to the same use and category. A tote and a crossbody are both bags, but only compare them once their purpose is the same.

Step 2 — Align the option

Match color, size, version, material, and any other selectable option. If one is black and the other is brown, the color has to line up before the rest of the comparison means anything.

Step 3 — Align the quantity

Separate a single item from a set, and a two-piece set from a three-piece set. Quantity changes what you are actually comparing.

Step 4 — Align the included parts

Confirm what is explicitly included, what is only shown in images, and what is an optional add-on. Included parts change the value and the comparison.

Step 5 — Check the current page context

Re-read the option against the page you actually opened. The selected color, size, and quantity on the current page are the live facts, not the label remembered from the sheet.

Manifest ruleIf the fields line up, you are comparing like with like. If they do not, you are comparing two different options and the gap is not meaningful.
